# **SimpliCARE Human SOP**

Version: 1.0  
Classification: Agency Operating Procedure  
Status: Draft for Review  
Owner: SimpliCreative  
Primary Users: SimpliCreative Strategy, Project Management, Website Support, Development, and Client Success Team

## **Purpose**

SimpliCARE is SimpliCreative’s ongoing website and digital-foundation support engagement.

Its purpose is to keep the client’s website useful, accurate, technically healthy, measurable, and aligned with the organization’s changing marketing needs.

CARE is not simply a queue of website requests.

The team should help clients maintain the digital foundation, make thoughtful improvements, understand marketing performance, identify opportunities, and use available evidence to determine what deserves attention next.

The governing principle is:

`MAINTAIN THE FOUNDATION. IMPROVE IT WITH EVIDENCE.`

## **1\. Activate the CARE Relationship**

Before ongoing work begins, confirm exactly what SimpliCreative is responsible for.

The team should understand the current website, approved strategy where available, messaging, technology, measurement requirements, known limitations, client stakeholders, partner responsibilities, and unresolved post-launch items.

| Confirm | Why It Matters |
| ----- | ----- |
| CARE agreement and scope | Defines what SimpliCreative owns |
| Primary client contact | Clarifies communication |
| Decision maker | Clarifies approval |
| Website and technology | Establishes current environment |
| Hosting ownership | Clarifies technical responsibility |
| CMS ownership | Clarifies implementation responsibility |
| Analytics and measurement | Defines available evidence |
| CRM and downstream systems | Defines available business-outcome evidence |
| Existing strategy | Protects strategic alignment |
| Existing partners | Prevents responsibility overlap |
| Known limitations | Preserves context |
| Current priorities | Establishes initial focus |
| Required access | Allows delivery |

CARE should not begin with unclear responsibility.

---

## **2\. Capture the Request or Opportunity**

CARE work may begin because the client requests something or because SimpliCreative identifies an opportunity.

For meaningful requests, understand:

What does the client want changed?

Why?

What outcome is the change intended to support?

Is there a deadline?

What systems or partners are involved?

Does the change affect another part of the marketing ecosystem?

Routine requests do not need extensive discovery.

A team-member bio update, event-date change, approved copy correction, document replacement, or similar maintenance request can usually move directly into execution.

---

## **3\. Classify the Work**

Use this framework before beginning work that is not obviously routine.

| Work Type | Example | What To Do |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Routine Maintenance | Copy change, team update, image replacement | Execute through normal workflow |
| Technical Maintenance | Plugin issue, form failure, compatibility problem | Route to technical owner |
| Content Implementation | New article, approved page content, resource | Implement after required approval |
| Campaign Support | Landing page, event page, campaign updates | Confirm objective and requirements |
| Measurement | Analytics, events, CRM tracking, reporting | Confirm measurement question and data |
| Optimization | Conversion, messaging, discoverability, performance improvement | Require evidence or defined hypothesis |
| Strategic Change | New audience, positioning, offer, buyer journey, major CTA change | Route to strategist |
| Scope Addition | Major functionality, redesign, integration | Commercial review |
| Separate Strategic Project | Major messaging, IA, Blueprint-level work | Scope separately |

The practical rule is:

If the client is changing how an approved strategy is executed, CARE can usually continue.

If the client is changing what the organization is saying, to whom, why it matters, what buyers should do, or how success is defined, involve the strategist.

---

## **4\. Prioritize the Work**

CARE should not simply operate first-in, first-out.

Consider business impact, buyer impact, technical risk, urgency, campaign timing, dependencies, available evidence, and effort.

| Priority Type | Typical Example |
| ----- | ----- |
| Critical | Outage, broken business-critical form, major functionality issue |
| High | Time-sensitive campaign, material conversion issue, important client requirement |
| Standard | Routine website or content improvement |
| Planned Optimization | Evidence-based improvement that can be scheduled |
| Enhancement | Useful but not important enough to displace higher-value work |

Do not allow easy tasks to continually crowd out work that matters more.

---

## **5\. Define the Work Before Starting**

For anything beyond a routine change, make sure the responsible person understands:

What are we changing?

Why?

What outcome should it support?

What approved strategy or decision governs it?

Who owns the work?

Who approves it?

What systems or partners are involved?

What dependencies exist?

How will we know it is complete?

Larger CARE initiatives may be managed as defined mini-projects with their own objective, requirements, owner, timeline, approvals, and QA.

---

## **6\. Execute the Work**

Execute according to the approved scope and requirements.

Depending on the CARE agreement, work may include website updates, landing pages, content implementation, forms, integrations, CMS support, technical maintenance, troubleshooting, analytics support, measurement, SEO implementation, AEO/GEO implementation, accessibility improvements, performance improvements, and conversion improvements.

AI may support retrieval, synthesis, first drafts, comparison, analysis, and documentation.

Humans remain responsible for final implementation decisions.

---

## **7\. Handle Content and Campaigns in Context**

Before publishing substantive content, confirm it is accurate, appropriately approved, and consistent with governing messaging and editorial standards.

AI may help draft content where included in scope.

Human review is required before publication.

Never invent testimonials, outcomes, statistics, credentials, guarantees, legal claims, regulatory claims, capabilities, or other factual assertions.

For campaign-related work, understand the audience, offer, message, traffic source, conversion action, measurement requirements, timing, and partner responsibilities.

The website should support the campaign rather than operate as an isolated production task.

If the campaign strategy itself is materially unresolved, involve the strategist.

---

## **8\. Maintain the Measurement Foundation**

CARE should protect the systems required to understand meaningful marketing performance.

Depending on the client, relevant systems may include:

| Source | Potential Role |
| ----- | ----- |
| GA4 | Website acquisition, engagement, behavior, and conversion activity |
| Google Search Console | Search visibility, queries, pages, clicks, impressions, CTR, position |
| HubSpot or other CRM | Contacts, qualified demand, opportunities, pipeline, lifecycle progression |
| YouTube | Video discovery, viewing, engagement, and content performance |
| Paid platforms | Campaign performance where included |
| Email/Marketing Automation | Email and lifecycle activity where relevant |
| Business systems | Donations, registrations, enrollment, appointments, applications, or other outcomes |
| Fathom | Client conversation and business context |
| Other approved systems | Client-specific evidence |

Not every client will have every source.

Use the sources that are available, relevant, and sufficiently reliable.

Installed analytics is not the same as meaningful measurement.

---

## **9\. Use Measurement to Guide Improvement**

Review data in the context of the client’s objectives.

Relevant evidence may include conversions, forms, analytics events, search behavior, CRM progression, campaign tracking, video performance, and downstream business activity.

Do not treat traffic or engagement alone as proof of business performance.

Ask:

What is the client trying to accomplish?

What changed?

What evidence do we have?

What does the evidence support?

What does it not support?

What deserves further investigation?

When data quality is weak, say so.

When attribution cannot support causation or ROI, do not claim it.

---

## **10\. Create Marketing Performance Intelligence**

Where included in the CARE engagement, synthesize the client’s available marketing data into a Marketing Performance Intelligence Report.

The purpose is not to reproduce dashboards.

The purpose is to help the marketing leader understand what happened, what appears to be working, what remains uncertain, how marketing is contributing, and what deserves attention next.

The report may synthesize:

GA4.

Google Search Console.

HubSpot or another CRM.

YouTube.

Paid media where included.

Email or marketing automation.

Business-outcome systems.

Fathom client-call context.

Previous reporting periods.

Relevant campaign and initiative context.

### **Recommended Report Structure**

| Section | Purpose |
| ----- | ----- |
| Executive Summary | Explain the most important story |
| Objectives | Reconnect performance to what the client is trying to accomplish |
| Reporting Period | Establish the comparison window |
| Data Sources | Make the evidence base visible |
| Data Quality & Limitations | Explain important constraints |
| Key KPIs | Surface meaningful measures |
| Website Performance | Explain acquisition, engagement, and conversion |
| Search Performance | Explain discoverability and search demand |
| CRM / Business Outcomes | Connect activity downstream where defensible |
| Content / Video / Campaign Performance | Explain relevant channel contribution |
| Cross-Channel Findings | Identify patterns across systems |
| Important Changes | Explain what changed during the period |
| Opportunities | Identify where attention may create value |
| Recommended Priorities | Recommend what deserves attention next |

AI may perform the first-pass synthesis.

The responsible human reviews the interpretation before client delivery.

---

## **11\. Create the Executive Impact Deck**

The Executive Impact Deck is a separate output from the full Marketing Performance Intelligence Report.

Its primary audience is executive leadership or the client’s board.

It should help the marketing director demonstrate marketing’s impact and explain what leadership should understand about the next period.

The Executive Deck should derive from the validated intelligence report.

It should not independently reinterpret raw data or introduce new conclusions.

A useful narrative is:

`OBJECTIVES → WHAT CHANGED → EVIDENCE OF IMPACT → WHAT WE LEARNED → NEXT-QUARTER OPPORTUNITIES`

### **Executive Deck Focus**

| Question | Deck Should Answer |
| ----- | ----- |
| What was marketing trying to accomplish? | Relevant objectives |
| What changed? | Meaningful performance movement |
| What impact can we demonstrate? | Evidence-supported contribution |
| What can we not yet prove? | Measurement or attribution limitations |
| What did we learn? | Important insight |
| What should leadership know? | Business relevance |
| Where is the opportunity? | Next-period focus |
| What should happen next? | Recommended priorities |

The deck should be concise, executive-friendly, and visually aligned with the client’s brand.

Do not turn it into a shortened analytics dashboard.

Human review is required before delivery.

## **12\. Identify Discoverability and Content Opportunities**

CARE may use Google Search Console and other approved evidence to identify opportunities for content and discoverability improvements.

Start with actual client search evidence.

Useful signals may include:

High-impression, low-click queries.

Non-branded search demand.

Relevant queries where the client already has some visibility.

Question-based searches.

Emerging query themes.

Queries connected to priority services or programs.

Pages receiving impressions but weak engagement.

Queries without an appropriate destination page.

Content already attracting qualified conversions where downstream evidence exists.

AI may cluster related queries, classify likely intent, compare queries with existing content, and suggest potential topics.

### **Content Opportunity Record**

| Field | Purpose |
| ----- | ----- |
| Topic | Recommended subject |
| Search Evidence | Queries and performance supporting it |
| Intent | What the searcher appears to need |
| Relevant Audience | Who the content serves |
| Existing Content | Whether something already addresses the need |
| Opportunity Type | New / Improve / Consolidate |
| Business Relevance | Connection to client objectives |
| Recommended Format | Guide / FAQ / comparison / article / resource / landing page |
| Query Cluster | Primary search theme |
| Supporting Queries | Related searches |
| Suggested Angle | Recommended editorial direction |
| Proof / Expertise Needed | Client expertise required |
| External Research Needed | Additional validation required |
| CTA | Appropriate next action |
| Priority | Relative importance |
| Human Review | Required |

High search volume or impressions alone do not make a topic strategically valuable.

The recommendation should also consider audience relevance, business relevance, buyer intent, existing content, available expertise, and the client’s priorities.

GSC evidence, client evidence, external research, AI interpretation, and strategist recommendations should remain distinguishable.

---

## **13\. Optimize From Evidence**

Optimization should begin with evidence or a clearly stated question.

Use:

`OBSERVATION → HYPOTHESIS → CHANGE → MEASUREMENT → LEARNING`

For example:

“Qualified visitors are reaching the service page but rarely continuing to consultation. We believe the page may not provide enough proof before the CTA.”

That is a hypothesis.

It is not a validated root cause until evidence supports that conclusion.

Potential CARE optimization may involve conversion paths, proof, messaging clarity, discoverability, content, performance, accessibility, measurement, or another approved area.

Do not make changes simply because a generic best practice recommends them.

---

## **14\. Watch for Strategic Drift**

Over time, small website changes can gradually alter the strategy.

Watch for changes involving audience, positioning, messaging, offers, buyer journey, CTAs, information architecture, content priorities, discoverability, measurement, or claims.

If the organization intentionally changes strategy, update the relevant approved context.

If the drift is accidental or unclear, involve the strategist.

Do not preserve outdated strategy merely because it was once approved.

Do not silently rewrite strategy through accumulated small requests.

---

## **15\. Manage Scope**

CARE is an ongoing relationship.

It is not unlimited capacity.

| Situation | Route |
| ----- | ----- |
| Routine work inside agreed responsibility | CARE |
| Larger but appropriate CARE initiative | Defined mini-project |
| Material additional responsibility | Commercial review |
| Major strategic work | Blueprint or separately scoped strategy |
| Major custom implementation | SimpliFoundation or separate project |
| Significant productized build | Evaluate appropriate website offer |

Large redesigns, major integrations, extensive custom development, substantial messaging strategy, and major IA restructuring should not disappear into CARE.

---

## **16\. Work With Other Partners**

CARE clients may work with paid-media agencies, PR firms, SEO providers, CRM consultants, internal teams, content partners, and other specialists.

Clarify who owns what.

SimpliCreative does not need to own every channel.

The objective is to make the digital foundation work effectively with the broader marketing ecosystem.

---

## **17\. Use AI as Support**

AI may help the CARE team:

Retrieve approved client context.

Summarize client history.

Organize requests.

Prepare implementation briefs.

Synthesize GA4, GSC, CRM, YouTube, and other approved data.

Use Fathom transcripts as contextual evidence.

Generate first drafts of Marketing Performance Intelligence Reports.

Generate first drafts of Executive Impact Decks.

Analyze GSC data.

Cluster search queries.

Suggest content opportunities.

Compare current website content with approved messaging.

Draft content.

Identify potential strategic drift.

Support QA.

Document meaningful decisions.

AI does not independently determine causation, certify ROI, approve strategic conclusions, choose final priorities, communicate conclusions to executives, approve scope, publish production changes, or deliver reports without human review.

---

## **18\. Keep Operations in the Right Systems**

Use monday.com for tasks, owners, priorities, deadlines, and execution status.

Use Figma for design artifacts.

Use MarkUp.io for contextual website feedback.

Use WordPress and approved development environments for implementation.

Use relevant analytics and CRM platforms as their appropriate systems of record.

Use the Second Brain for durable intelligence.

The Second Brain should preserve why something mattered, what was learned, what was decided, and what should be remembered.

It should not duplicate routine project activity.

---

## **19\. Apply the Right Level of QA**

Not every CARE task needs the same QA process.

| Change Type | Appropriate QA |
| ----- | ----- |
| Simple text correction | Content and visual check |
| Image/resource update | Content, link, and responsive check |
| New page | Content, responsive, links, discoverability, accessibility as applicable |
| Landing page | Content, CTA, form, analytics, responsive, accessibility |
| Form change | Submission, routing, confirmation, CRM, analytics |
| Integration change | Functional and regression testing |
| Measurement change | Event, platform, and reporting validation |
| Technical update | Technical and regression QA |
| Strategic conversion change | Strategic fidelity plus technical QA |
| Intelligence Report | Data, calculations, definitions, interpretation, certainty, attribution |
| Executive Deck | Traceability to approved intelligence, clarity, certainty, brand |
| Content Opportunities | GSC evidence, intent, business relevance, strategy alignment |

Use judgment based on actual risk.

Humans approve QA.

---

## **20\. Publish Changes**

Production changes remain human-controlled.

Before a meaningful change goes live, confirm required approvals and QA.

For higher-risk changes, consider rollback or recovery requirements.

AI does not independently publish production changes.

---

## **21\. Review the Relationship Regularly**

CARE should include the appropriate review cadence for the purchased engagement.

The review should focus on:

What changed?

What was completed?

What is the evidence showing?

What did we learn?

What deserves attention next?

What is the client planning?

What constraints exist?

Are current priorities still aligned with the client’s objectives?

Do not turn the review into a vanity-metric presentation.

The purpose is better decision-making.

---

## **22\. Capture Outcomes**

When a meaningful change has enough evidence to evaluate, capture the result.

| Capture | Description |
| ----- | ----- |
| Change | What was implemented |
| Reason | Why it was changed |
| Measurement | What was evaluated |
| Period | Relevant timeframe |
| Observation | What happened |
| Data Quality | Reliability of available evidence |
| Other Factors | What else may have influenced the result |
| Supported Conclusion | What evidence reasonably supports |
| Remaining Uncertainty | What cannot yet be concluded |
| Next Action | What should happen next |

A positive result does not automatically prove causation.

A negative result does not automatically prove the recommendation was wrong.

---

## **23\. Preserve Useful Client Intelligence**

Preserve durable information such as approved messaging changes, new offers, stakeholder changes, durable client preferences, measurement definitions, technology changes, recurring constraints, meaningful decisions, important technical choices, and validated outcomes.

Ask:

“Will knowing this later help us understand the client or make a better decision?”

If not, leave it in the operational system.

---

## **24\. Learn Without Overgeneralizing**

Repeated CARE work may reveal patterns worth considering across the agency.

Capture those as candidate learning.

Do not turn one client’s optimization, preference, or outcome into an agency rule.

Reusable methodology requires broader evidence and deliberate human approval.

---

## **25\. Transition or Exit CARE**

When CARE ends or changes, document what remains open and who owns it.

Confirm:

Outstanding work.

Unresolved issues.

Approvals.

Website ownership.

Hosting.

Maintenance.

Analytics.

Measurement.

Integrations.

System access.

Documentation.

Known risks.

Ongoing partner responsibilities.

The client should understand what SimpliCreative owns after the transition, if anything.

## **CARE Success Standard**

CARE is ongoing, so success is not measured by reaching a final launch or completing the largest possible number of tasks.

The engagement is working when the client’s digital foundation remains aligned, useful, technically healthy, responsive to changing marketing needs, and increasingly informed by evidence.

It should also help the marketing leader make marketing’s impact visible to the people responsible for evaluating that investment.

The standard is not:

“Did we complete every request?”

It is:

“Did we help the client keep their digital foundation working as their organization and marketing evolved, understand what the evidence is telling them, demonstrate meaningful marketing impact, and make better decisions about what should happen next?”
