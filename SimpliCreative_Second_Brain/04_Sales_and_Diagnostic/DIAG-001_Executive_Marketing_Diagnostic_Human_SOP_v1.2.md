# **Purpose**

This SOP explains how the SimpliCreative team prepares for, conducts, documents, and routes an Executive Marketing Diagnostic.

The Executive Marketing Diagnostic is a complimentary 25 to 30-minute strategic conversation for qualified prospects.

It is not a generic free website audit.

It is designed to help us understand the prospect’s situation, connect what they are experiencing to the available evidence, determine whether SimpliCreative is a fit, and decide whether deeper strategic analysis is warranted.

The governing principle is:

DIAGNOSE BEFORE PRESCRIBE

The Diagnostic should create real clarity without pretending we know more than the available evidence supports.

# **1\. Understand the Job of the Diagnostic**

The Diagnostic has three jobs.

First, determine whether the prospect and problem are a fit for SimpliCreative.

Second, understand what their Scorecard results and available website evidence may be telling us about the problem.

Third, determine the most responsible next step.

The Diagnostic is not intended to:

Complete a comprehensive audit.

Determine definitive root causes.

Produce a full marketing strategy.

Design the solution.

Prove the prospect needs a Blueprint.

Prove the prospect needs a new website.

Provide unpaid implementation planning.

The goal is to understand the problem well enough to know what needs to be learned or done next.

# **2\. Know How Someone Reaches the Diagnostic**

A prospect will typically reach the Diagnostic after completing the Lean Marketing Scorecard™ and meeting the appropriate qualification criteria.

They may also arrive through an existing relationship, referral, direct conversation, or another approved path.

Regardless of source, the Diagnostic should not begin as a cold conversation when useful information already exists.

Use what we know.

Do not make the prospect repeat information simply because it came through another system.

# **3\. Prepare Before the Call**

Before the Diagnostic, review the Executive Diagnostic Brief.

The brief should consolidate the most useful information from the prospect record, Scorecard, CRM, relationship history, approved public research, website evidence, and FAST pre-scan.

You should be able to quickly understand:

| What to Know | Why It Matters |
| :---- | :---- |
| Prospect and organization | Basic context |
| What they say is wrong | Their view of the problem |
| Why now | Business pressure or trigger |
| Scorecard results | Their self-assessment |
| Existing relationship history | What we already know |
| Preliminary website evidence | What we can observe |
| Potential ecosystem disconnects | Where friction may exist |
| Working hypotheses | What may be worth investigating |
| Contradicting evidence | What challenges those hypotheses |
| Missing information | What we need to learn |
| Fit signals | Why this may be appropriate for SimpliCreative |
| Risk or non-fit signals | What deserves caution |
| Questions to validate | What the conversation needs to uncover |

 

The brief is preparation, not a script.

# **4\. Treat Pre-Call Research as a Starting Point**

Automated and AI-supported research can help us enter the conversation informed.

It cannot tell us what the prospect thinks, why leadership is concerned, what internal politics exist, how purchase decisions are made, or what has already been tried unless we have evidence for those things.

A CMS detector can tell us something about technology.

It cannot tell us whether the marketing team has sufficient autonomy.

An accessibility scan can identify possible issues.

It cannot determine legal compliance.

An analytics tag can indicate that a tool is installed.

It cannot tell us whether measurement is meaningful.

A website observation can reveal visible friction.

It cannot automatically establish the root cause.

Use research to improve the questions you ask, not to eliminate the need to ask them.

# **5\. Enter With Hypotheses, Not Answers**

Before the call, you may have ideas about what is happening.

Keep them as working hypotheses.

For example:

The prospect may believe they have a traffic problem.

The available evidence may suggest the more important question is whether existing attention is converting into meaningful action.

That does not mean we announce:

“You don't have a traffic problem. You have a conversion problem.”

Instead, investigate.

The purpose of the Diagnostic is to test our initial read against the prospect's reality.

# **6\. Open the Conversation**

Start by establishing what prompted the conversation.

Understand:

What is happening?

Why does it matter now?

What are they trying to improve?

What has leadership noticed?

What have they already tried?

What would a better outcome look like?

Keep the opening conversational.

Do not turn the call into an interrogation or questionnaire walkthrough.

The prospect should feel that we are trying to understand the business problem, not qualify them into a predetermined offer.

# **7\. Confirm Fit as You Listen**

The best SimpliCreative fit is generally an established service organization with a lean internal marketing team managing meaningful complexity.

Listen for conditions such as:

Active marketing.

Multiple channels.

Multiple stakeholders or partners.

A website that plays an important role in marketing.

Execution friction.

Measurement friction.

Fragmented activity.

Pressure to demonstrate impact.

Strategic complexity.

A need for execution, not merely advice.

“Lean” describes the relationship between the team's capacity and its responsibilities.

Do not disqualify an organization simply because the marketing team is above or below an arbitrary headcount.

Complexity matters more than company size alone.

# **8\. Watch for Non-Fit**

Potential non-fit situations include organizations that primarily need inexpensive production work, isolated tactical execution, or a predetermined tactic regardless of evidence.

Other concerns include:

Very early-stage organizations without a validated offer, audience, or meaningful marketing investment.

Primarily ecommerce or product businesses where the core challenge is merchandising, inventory, or transactional commerce outside our expertise.

Prospects with no realistic authority, capacity, or investment to act.

Work requiring expertise SimpliCreative does not provide.

Prospects seeking guarantees the evidence cannot support.

Do not force a fit because the prospect has a website problem.

# **9\. Use the Scorecard as a Conversation Tool**

The Lean Marketing Scorecard™ evaluates four areas:

Discoverability.

Credibility.

Performance.

Agility.

The score is not the diagnosis.

Use the results to understand how the prospect sees their own marketing environment.

Ask where the result feels accurate.

Ask where it surprises them.

Ask what they believe is contributing to the result.

Ask what has already been attempted.

The value is in the conversation the score creates, not the number itself.

# **10\. Use FAST to Explore the Website**

FAST gives us a practical way to discuss the website without reducing the conversation to visual design.

## **Flexible**

Can the marketing team use the website effectively?

Explore whether they can make routine changes, create campaign experiences, update content, and move without unnecessary technical friction.

Do not infer autonomy solely from the CMS.

## **Accessible**

Can people effectively access and use the experience?

We may surface observable or automated accessibility concerns.

Do not diagnose legal compliance.

## **Strategic**

Does the website support the business and marketing strategy?

Explore audience alignment, messaging, discoverability, credibility, buyer education, conversion, content, offers, and the role the website plays across marketing.

## **Trackable**

Can the organization understand what meaningful actions happen after people arrive?

Explore analytics, conversions, CRM connection, reporting, and leadership visibility.

Do not assume that installed tracking equals meaningful measurement.

# **11\. Look Beyond the Website**

The website is the connective layer, not the entire marketing ecosystem.

If the prospect describes a website problem, understand what surrounds it.

Explore relevant relationships between:

Business strategy.

Audience.

Positioning.

Messaging.

Brand.

Discoverability.

Demand generation.

Content.

PR.

Paid media.

Conversion.

Sales or enrollment.

Lifecycle.

Technology.

Measurement.

People.

Partners.

The visible website symptom may be created elsewhere in the ecosystem.

Likewise, the website may be preventing otherwise strong marketing from compounding.

# **12\. Separate the Symptom From the Cause**

Prospects will often arrive with a solution already in mind.

“We need more leads.”

“We need a new website.”

“We need SEO.”

“Our paid ads aren't working.”

“We need better content.”

“We need to show up in AI search.”

“We need better attribution.”

Treat those statements seriously.

The fire is real.

But the proposed solution may or may not address what is creating the fire.

Ask enough questions to understand what is actually happening before discussing what should change.

# **13\. Ask Questions That Change Understanding**

You do not need to ask every prepared question.

Prioritize questions that help determine:

What the prospect believes is happening.

What evidence supports that belief.

What business outcome is affected.

Where the friction appears.

What has already been attempted.

What changed.

What other channels or partners are involved.

What happens after someone reaches the website.

How success is currently measured.

What leadership expects.

What the marketing team can realistically execute.

What remains unknown.

A good Diagnostic question changes the quality of the conversation.

# **14\. Do Not Overdiagnose**

The Diagnostic gives us limited evidence.

Be careful with statements such as:

“Your root cause is...”

“This is definitely what's happening...”

“The redesign will fix...”

“Your SEO is the problem...”

“Your positioning is wrong...”

“You need a Blueprint...”

Instead, use language that reflects the evidence:

“What I'm seeing so far suggests...”

“One thing I would want to validate more deeply is...”

“There may be a disconnect between...”

“This appears to be showing up in...”

“The question I would want answered before deciding what to change is...”

Evidence determines certainty.

# **15\. Reflect the Pattern Back**

Near the end of the conversation, help the prospect see what appears to connect the issues they described.

This is where SimpliCreative's point of view becomes valuable.

You might connect:

Paid traffic with landing-page alignment.

PR attention with website credibility and measurement.

Content with the buyer journey.

Traffic with qualified conversion.

Marketing activity with leadership reporting.

Vendor fragmentation with execution friction.

Website limitations with marketer autonomy.

The purpose is not to impress the prospect with a diagnosis.

It is to help them see the problem more clearly than they did before the conversation.

# **16\. Determine What Is Known and What Is Still Unknown**

Before recommending a next step, mentally separate:

What we know from evidence.

What the prospect has told us.

What appears likely.

What remains uncertain.

What would need deeper investigation.

This distinction is especially important when deciding whether the situation requires a Blueprint or whether implementation can already be responsibly defined.

# **17\. Decide Whether Strategy Is Sufficiently Established**

The Diagnostic does not automatically lead to a Blueprint.

Ask whether the information needed to define implementation is already sufficiently established.

Relevant areas may include:

Business objective.

Website objective.

ICP.

Positioning.

Differentiation.

Offers.

Messaging.

Buyer journey.

Conversion.

Content.

Information architecture.

Discoverability.

Measurement.

CMS.

CRM and integrations.

Accessibility requirements.

Stakeholders.

Partners.

Implementation priorities.

Success criteria.

Material unknowns.

Not every field needs a lengthy strategy document.

The question is whether unresolved strategic questions would prevent us from responsibly defining the implementation.

# **18\. Choose the Appropriate Route**

At the end of the Diagnostic, the human determines the next route.

| Situation | Likely Route |
| :---- | :---- |
| Material strategic uncertainty remains | SimpliBlueprint |
| Strategy is sufficiently established and custom implementation can be defined | Direct-to-Foundation Scoping |
| Strategy/content are established and the project genuinely fits the productized implementation model | Templated WordPress |
| Prospect is potentially a future fit but not ready | Guidance / Nurture |
| SimpliCreative is not the right provider | Referral / Close |

 

The route follows the evidence.

Do not make the evidence fit the offer.

# **19\. Explain the Gap Between the Diagnostic and Blueprint**

When Blueprint is the appropriate next step, explain why deeper work is required.

A useful framing is:

“What we've identified today is where the friction appears to be showing up. What we can't responsibly determine from a complimentary Diagnostic conversation is the root cause, the priority order, and the best implementation path. That's what the deeper strategic engagement is designed to answer.”

The limitation exists because the available evidence is limited.

It does not exist because SimpliCreative intentionally withholds useful information from a free conversation.

Be generous with insight.

Be disciplined about conclusions.

# **20\. Explain Direct-to-Foundation When Appropriate**

If strategy is already sufficiently established, do not manufacture uncertainty to sell a Blueprint.

Explain that the next step is to define the custom implementation requirements and scope.

The purpose of Direct-to-Foundation Scoping is to confirm that we know enough to responsibly build what is needed.

If material strategic uncertainty appears during scoping, we can address the routing decision then.

# **21\. Explain Templated WordPress When Appropriate**

The Templated WordPress route is appropriate only when the strategic foundation and content are sufficiently established and the implementation genuinely fits the productized model.

Do not route a strategically complex custom engagement into the template offer simply because the prospect wants a lower-cost website.

Likewise, do not route a straightforward implementation into Foundation simply because Foundation is the larger engagement.

Match the solution to the complexity.

# **22\. Do Not Price From the Diagnostic Unless the Route Supports It**

The Diagnostic helps determine fit and route.

It is not permission to invent scope or pricing in real time.

Use approved pricing and scoping processes for the selected engagement.

If scope is not sufficiently defined, say so.

The team should not make a commercial commitment before understanding the level of responsibility SimpliCreative would be assuming.

# **23\. Close the Conversation With Clarity**

By the end of the Diagnostic, the prospect should understand:

What we heard.

Where the friction appears to be showing up.

What we can responsibly say based on the available evidence.

What still needs to be understood.

Whether SimpliCreative appears to be a fit.

What the next step is, if there is one.

Avoid manufacturing urgency.

Clarity is the goal.

# **24\. Document the Diagnostic**

After the call, preserve the information that will matter later.

AI can use the transcript and human notes to prepare the Diagnostic Record.

Capture:

What the prospect said is happening.

Why now.

Business and marketing objectives.

Desired outcome.

Relevant Scorecard context.

Important facts learned.

Important website or ecosystem observations.

Working hypotheses.

Evidence supporting those hypotheses.

Evidence contradicting them.

Unknowns.

Fit indicators.

Risk indicators.

Strategic questions requiring deeper work.

Decisions made.

Next-step route.

The human validates the material conclusions before they become durable client intelligence.

# **25\. Update Sales Intelligence**

The Sales Intelligence Worksheet should reflect what we learned from the actual conversation.

The post-call record may now include:

Validated facts.

Corrected assumptions.

New stakeholder information.

Purchase context.

Business pressure.

Strategic complexity.

Known constraints.

New evidence.

Changed hypotheses.

Fit.

Blueprint readiness where relevant.

Commercial readiness.

The conversation should improve the intelligence record.

It should not simply produce a meeting summary.

# **26\. Separate Blueprint Readiness From Commercial Readiness**

A prospect can be an excellent fit for a Blueprint without being ready to receive a proposal.

Blueprint readiness asks:

“Would deeper strategic work be valuable and appropriate?”

Commercial readiness asks:

“Do we have enough information and alignment to responsibly advance a commercial engagement?”

Those are different questions.

A prospect requesting a proposal does not automatically mean the opportunity is commercially ready.

Likewise, a strategically appropriate prospect may need another human conversation before a proposal is appropriate.

# **27\. Use AI as Preparation and Documentation Support**

During the Diagnostic, AI is useful for:

Assembling known prospect context.

Analyzing Scorecard results.

Organizing approved public research.

Performing preliminary FAST evidence collection.

Surfacing possible ecosystem disconnects.

Generating working hypotheses.

Identifying missing information.

Preparing questions.

Creating the Executive Diagnostic Brief.

Processing the transcript.

Updating the Sales Intelligence Worksheet.

Preparing the Diagnostic Record.

Checking whether conclusions exceed the evidence.

AI helps us enter the conversation better prepared and leave it with better memory.

# **28\. Know What AI Must Not Decide**

AI does not make the final decision about:

Prospect fit.

Root cause.

The final route.

Whether a redesign is required.

Final scope.

Final pricing.

Proposal readiness.

Strategic conclusions.

Accessibility compliance.

SEO/AEO/GEO outcomes.

Whether a prospect will buy.

Whether a prospect trusts us.

Whether the marketing problem has been solved.

Those require human judgment.

# **29\. Protect Against False Certainty**

Remember these practical distinctions:

A Scorecard result is not a root cause.

An automated scan is not a validated conclusion.

A public signal is not the prospect's motivation.

A job title is not proof of purchase authority.

A polite conversation is not proof of trust.

A proposal request is not proof of commercial readiness.

Correlation is not causation.

AI confidence is not evidence.

The Diagnostic should make uncertainty more useful, not hide it.

# **30\. Know When the Diagnostic Is Complete**

The Diagnostic is complete when we understand enough to make a responsible routing decision.

Before closing the record, confirm:

| Closeout Check | Complete |
| :---- | :---- |
| Prospect's stated problem captured | Required |
| Why-now context understood | Required |
| Desired outcome understood | Required |
| Relevant Scorecard context reviewed | Where available |
| Preliminary FAST evidence considered | Where relevant |
| Important ecosystem context explored | Required |
| Material hypotheses documented | Required |
| Evidence limitations preserved | Required |
| Important unknowns documented | Required |
| Fit evaluated by a human | Required |
| Next route determined by a human | Required |
| Diagnostic Record validated | Required |
| Sales Intelligence updated | Required |

 

The Diagnostic does not need to answer every strategic question.

If it did, we would have performed the Blueprint.

# **31\. Diagnostic Output Architecture**

The original human Diagnostic sequence above remains the governing operating procedure.

The standard Diagnostic output architecture is intentionally small.

| ID / Record | Standard Output | Audience | Primary Production | Human Authority | Typical Format |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| System Record | Sales Intelligence Record | Internal | AI-assisted persistent intelligence | Strategist validates material updates | Structured system object |
| GS-DIAG-001 | Executive Diagnostic Brief | Internal | AI-produced first draft from approved pre-call context | Strategist reviews before call | Editable DOCX |
| Source Evidence | Transcript \+ Strategist Notes | Internal | Human conversation evidence; AI may transcribe and extract | Strategist validates material corrections and commitments | Transcript \+ notes |
| System Record | Hypothesis Reconciliation Record | Internal | AI-assisted reconciliation of pre-call hypotheses against call evidence | Strategist may correct or override | Structured system object |
| GS-DIAG-002 | Executive Diagnostic Record | Internal formal record | AI-produced first draft from transcript, evidence, and updated Sales Intelligence | Strategist approves conclusions, fit, and route | Editable DOCX |
| System Record | Readiness Decision Record | Internal | AI-assisted, human-led decision support | Strategist owns readiness and next route | Structured system object |
| GS-DIAG-003 | Executive Diagnostic Follow-Up | Prospect / Client | AI-produced first draft from the approved Diagnostic Record, route decision, and commitments | Human review required before send | Email / editable DOCX sample |

 

AI-generated does not mean AI-decided.

The strategist owns the actual Diagnostic conversation, the fit decision, the final route, the final strategic conclusion, proposal readiness, and the client-facing recommendation.

The transcript remains source evidence and should remain linked to the Diagnostic Record. It should not be replaced by an AI summary.

The Sales Intelligence Record is persistent. Do not create a disconnected second intelligence worksheet after the call. Update the same governed record so the system can reconstruct what changed.

# **32\. Hypothesis Reconciliation**

Pre-call hypotheses should not disappear when the call produces better evidence.

After the Diagnostic, reconcile each material hypothesis against what the prospect said, what the strategist observed, and any new evidence.

Preserve:

Pre-call hypothesis.

Pre-call evidence.

Contradicting evidence.

New call evidence.

Post-call status.

Validation basis.

Remaining unknowns.

Human correction or override.

Source references.

The preferred reasoning trace is:

HYPOTHESIS  
 → NEW EVIDENCE  
 → CONTRADICTING EVIDENCE  
 → RECONCILIATION  
 → UPDATED STATUS  
 → HUMAN REVIEW

Do not silently rewrite the original hypothesis so it appears that the system was correct from the beginning.

# **33\. Separate Route, Blueprint Fit, Commercial Readiness, and Proposal Readiness**

The Diagnostic already separates Blueprint readiness from commercial readiness.

For the operating system, preserve the distinction even more explicitly.

The human route decision asks:

“What is the most responsible next step based on the evidence?”

Blueprint strategic fit asks:

“Would deeper strategic work be valuable and appropriate?”

Commercial readiness asks:

“Do we have enough alignment, authority, timing, and commercial context to responsibly advance an engagement?”

Proposal readiness asks:

“Do we have enough validated information and human approval to put a specific engagement in front of the prospect?”

These states are related but not interchangeable.

Apply these rules:

ROUTE\_DECISION \!= COMMERCIAL\_READINESS  
 BLUEPRINT\_FIT \!= PROPOSAL\_READINESS  
 COMMERCIAL\_READINESS \!= PROPOSAL\_READINESS  
 DIAGNOSTIC\_COMPLETE \!= PROPOSAL\_READY  
 PROPOSAL\_REQUEST \!= PROPOSAL\_READINESS

A prospect may be a strong fit for SimpliBlueprint and still require another human conversation before a proposal is appropriate.

# **34\. Direct-to-Foundation Boundary**

Direct-to-Foundation means strategy appears sufficiently established to move into a separate Foundation Scoping process.

It does not mean the strategist fully scopes or prices a custom SimpliFoundation engagement during the complimentary Diagnostic.

The Diagnostic determines whether deeper strategy is required.

Foundation Scoping determines how an already-established strategy should be implemented.

Foundation Scoping should establish the implementation responsibilities, architecture, content, design, CMS, CRM and integrations, measurement, discoverability, accessibility, migration, functionality, stakeholders, partners, dependencies, complexity, delivery economics, and the recommended fixed investment.

Apply this rule:

DIRECT\_TO\_FOUNDATION \!= SCOPE\_AND\_PRICE\_FOUNDATION\_DURING\_DIAGNOSTIC

If material strategic uncertainty appears during Foundation Scoping, return the issue to strategic review.

# **35\. Diagnostic Follow-Up**

When an external follow-up is appropriate, use the Executive Diagnostic Follow-Up.

The follow-up should reflect what the prospect said, what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, the human-approved route, the immediate next action, and any commitments made.

It should not become a giant automated audit.

It should not introduce a new strategy.

It should not convert a working hypothesis into a proven root cause.

It should not imply proposal readiness unless proposal readiness has been separately human-approved.

Apply these rules:

DIAGNOSTIC\_FOLLOWUP\_MUST\_TRACE\_TO\_APPROVED\_DIAGNOSTIC\_RECORD  
 HYPOTHESIS\_MUST\_NOT\_BECOME\_CONCLUSION\_IN\_FOLLOWUP  
 FOLLOWUP\_MUST\_NOT\_INTRODUCE\_NEW\_STRATEGY  
 FOLLOWUP\_MUST\_NOT\_IMPLY\_PROPOSAL\_READINESS  
 CLIENT\_COMMITMENTS\_MUST\_MATCH\_CALL\_EVIDENCE  
 HUMAN\_APPROVAL\_REQUIRED\_BEFORE\_SEND

# **36\. Sales / Commercial Handoff**

The Diagnostic may inform a later Sales or commercial action.

It does not automatically authorize one.

The Diagnostic workflow ends with an evidence-backed human decision about fit, route, readiness, and next action.

When proposal readiness is separately human-approved, the downstream Sales workflow may generate the appropriate proposal.

Current standard commercial outputs may include:

A personalized SimpliBlueprint Proposal.

A Foundation Scoping Record when custom Foundation implementation requires scoping.

A personalized SimpliFoundation Proposal after fixed scope and human-approved pricing exist.

A Proposal Send / Next-Step Email after the proposal is approved for send.

A controlled agreement or SOW populated from approved commercial terms.

The Diagnostic does not author legal terms, approve pricing, or declare an opportunity Closed Won.

Apply these rules:

DIAGNOSTIC\_INTELLIGENCE\_MAY\_INFORM\_SALES  
 DIAGNOSTIC\_INTELLIGENCE\_DOES\_NOT\_AUTHORIZE\_COMMERCIAL\_ACTION  
 PROPOSAL\_SENT \!= ACCEPTED  
 VERBAL\_INTEREST \!= CLOSED\_WON  
 POLITENESS \!= COMMITMENT  
 SIGNED\_AGREEMENT\_REQUIRES\_SOURCE\_EVIDENCE  
 AI\_MAY\_DOCUMENT\_COMMERCIAL\_STATE  
 AI\_MAY\_NOT\_DECLARE\_CLOSED\_WON\_WITHOUT\_APPROVED\_EVIDENCE

The preferred boundary is:

DIAGNOSTIC  
 → HUMAN ROUTE \+ READINESS DECISION  
 → DIAGNOSTIC FOLLOW-UP WHEN APPROPRIATE  
 → SALES / COMMERCIAL WORKFLOW WHEN APPROPRIATE

The downstream commercial sequence is:

HUMAN-APPROVED ROUTE  
 → COMMERCIAL READINESS  
 → PROPOSAL READINESS  
 → PROPOSAL  
 → HUMAN COMMERCIAL REVIEW  
 → PROPOSAL SENT  
 → PROSPECT RESPONSE  
 → CONTROLLED AGREEMENT / SOW  
 → SIGNED AGREEMENT \+ APPROVED ACTIVATION CONDITION  
 → CLOSED WON  
 → DELIVERY ACTIVATION

# **37\. Diagnostic Output Checklist Rule**

A standard output is produced only when its trigger is satisfied and the required upstream context exists.

Missing context is not permission to infer.

AI may organize evidence, draft approved outputs, compare pre-call and post-call states, and prepare decision support.

AI may not make the final strategic, commercial, pricing, legal, or launch decision.

# **Executive Marketing Diagnostic at a Glance**

| Phase | Team Focus | Done When |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Prepare | Review Scorecard, Sales Intelligence, website evidence, and hypotheses | Strategist enters informed |
| Open | Understand the problem and why now | Business context is clear |
| Explore | Scorecard, FAST, ecosystem, objectives, measurement | Important friction is understood |
| Test | Compare prospect reality with working hypotheses | Initial assumptions are strengthened, changed, or challenged |
| Reflect | Connect the visible symptoms | Prospect gains useful clarity |
| Assess | Determine fit and remaining uncertainty | We know whether SimpliCreative can responsibly help |
| Route | Select Blueprint, Foundation scoping, Templated WordPress, nurture, or close | Next step matches the evidence |
| Document | Update Diagnostic and Sales Intelligence | Durable intelligence reflects what was actually learned |

 

The standard for a successful Executive Marketing Diagnostic is not:

“Did we convince the prospect to buy something?”

It is:

“Did we understand the problem well enough to help the prospect see it more clearly, determine whether SimpliCreative is the right fit, and recommend the most responsible next step without pretending the evidence tells us more than it does?”
