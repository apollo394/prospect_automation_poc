# **Team Guide**

Version: 1.0  
Classification: Internal Team Operating Guide  
Status: Final  
Owner: SimpliCreative  
Primary Users: SimpliCreative Team  
Purpose: Explain how the team should use the SimpliCreative AI Second Brain in day-to-day work

## **1\. What the Second Brain Is**

The SimpliCreative Second Brain is our shared AI Intelligence Layer.

It is designed to help the team find and use the knowledge SimpliCreative has already created, connect information across our work, prepare faster, preserve important decisions, and reduce the amount of context we have to reconstruct manually.

Think of it as shared organizational intelligence.

It should help us answer questions such as:

What do we know about this client?

What did we decide?

Why did we decide it?

What does our current strategy say?

What process should we follow?

What evidence do we have?

What is still uncertain?

What should I review before this meeting?

What happened after we implemented something?

The Second Brain supports our judgment.

It does not replace it.

## **2\. What the Second Brain Is Not**

The Second Brain is not the place where every piece of information automatically becomes truth.

It is not a replacement for human judgment.

It is not a replacement for monday.com, HubSpot, Google Drive, Fathom, or our other operating systems.

It is not permission to publish AI-generated work without review.

It is not permission to reuse one client's work for another client.

It is not a system that makes strategic decisions for us.

And it is not automatically correct just because its answer sounds confident.

## **3\. The Basic Rule**

When using the Second Brain:

`AI FINDS THE PATTERN. HUMANS DECIDE WHAT IT MEANS.`

AI can help us retrieve, organize, compare, summarize, draft, identify patterns, prepare recommendations, and surface gaps.

Humans remain accountable for what SimpliCreative believes, recommends, commits to, publishes, spends, and learns.

## **4\. Where Information Lives**

The Second Brain connects information from multiple systems, but those systems still have different jobs.

| System | Primary Job |
| ----- | ----- |
| Second Brain | Intelligence, context, reasoning, knowledge retrieval |
| HubSpot | Prospect, company, deal, lifecycle, and acquisition information |
| monday.com | Projects, tasks, responsibilities, and delivery |
| Google Drive | Documents and approved source files |
| Fathom | Meeting transcripts and recordings |
| ScoreApp | Lean Marketing Scorecard™ responses and results |
| Analytics Platforms | Performance evidence |
| Paid Platforms | Campaign evidence |
| DocuSign | Executed agreements |

Do not move information into a different system simply because the Second Brain can retrieve it.

## **5\. What You Can Ask the Second Brain**

The Second Brain should be useful for questions such as:

“What should I know before my meeting with this client?”

“Summarize the decisions from our last three calls.”

“What did we promise in the scope?”

“What does our current Blueprint process require here?”

“What evidence supports this recommendation?”

“Where did this client disagree with us?”

“What questions are still unresolved?”

“What does SimpliCreative currently say about this?”

“Compare the current performance with the previous period.”

“Prepare a first draft based on the approved strategy.”

“Show me the current pricing source.”

“Is this recommendation supported by evidence or are we making an inference?”

The more consequential the decision, the more important human review becomes.

## **6\. What Goes Into the Second Brain**

Useful organizational intelligence includes:

Approved strategy.

Current processes.

Client facts.

Client decisions.

Meeting intelligence.

Research.

Evidence.

Recommendations.

Project decisions.

Commercial decisions.

Performance findings.

Campaign tests.

Outcomes.

Lessons learned.

Not everything automatically becomes permanent SimpliCreative knowledge.

The system distinguishes between something that happened and something SimpliCreative has decided to believe or adopt.

## **7\. Client Knowledge**

Client information stays with the client.

A useful idea from one engagement does not automatically become something we can reuse for another client.

The Second Brain distinguishes among:

| Type | Meaning |
| ----- | ----- |
| Client Fact | Something known about the client |
| Client Preference | Something the client prefers |
| Client Decision | A decision made for that client |
| Client Outcome | What happened |
| Agency Observation | Something we noticed |
| Candidate Agency Learning | Something that may apply more broadly |
| Approved Agency Learning | Something SimpliCreative has deliberately adopted |

This distinction matters.

One client's preference is not automatically a SimpliCreative best practice.

One successful outcome is not automatically a universal rule.

## **8\. Protecting Client Information**

Never intentionally ask the Second Brain to use confidential information from one client for another client.

Do not use one client's deliverable as a copy bank for another.

If you see information from the wrong client in your context, stop.

Do not continue using the output.

Flag the issue immediately.

Cross-client information appearing where it does not belong is a system problem, not something to work around.

## **9\. Current vs. Old Information**

The Second Brain contains different kinds of sources.

Some are current.

Some are examples.

Some are external references.

Some are historical.

Some have been replaced.

You should not have to manually determine which version wins. The system is designed to handle that.

But if something looks wrong or outdated, flag it.

Do not assume the AI must know better because it retrieved the information.

## **10\. When Sources Conflict**

Sometimes two sources may disagree.

When that happens, the Second Brain should surface the conflict instead of quietly choosing one.

You may see something similar to:

`AGENCY_SOURCE_CONFLICT_REQUIRES_HUMAN_REVIEW`

That means a human needs to determine which source is correct.

Once resolved, the governing knowledge should be updated so the same ambiguity does not keep recurring.

## **11\. When the Second Brain Does Not Know**

The system is allowed to say it does not have enough authoritative information.

That is a feature.

You may see:

`MISSING_AUTHORITY_REQUIRES_HUMAN_INPUT`

Do not encourage the AI to “just make its best guess” when the missing information involves strategy, pricing, scope, policy, client commitments, or another consequential decision.

Resolve the missing knowledge instead.

## **12\. Evidence vs. Interpretation**

One of the most important habits when using the Second Brain is distinguishing what we know from what we think may be happening.

The system may identify something as:

Observed.

Reported.

Inferred.

Hypothesized.

Directional.

Validated.

Partially validated.

Contradicted.

Unsupported.

Unknown.

These labels matter.

For example, a client saying, “Our website is causing the problem,” is evidence that the client believes the website is causing the problem.

It is not automatically proof that the website caused the problem.

## **13\. Using AI for Strategy**

AI can help us:

Collect evidence.

Organize research.

Identify patterns.

Develop hypotheses.

Compare options.

Find contradictions.

Draft recommendations.

Prepare strategic materials.

AI should not independently determine SimpliCreative's final strategic position.

A strategist remains responsible for the conclusion.

Our operating principle remains:

`DIAGNOSE BEFORE YOU PRESCRIBE`

## **14\. Using AI for Client Deliverables**

AI can help create first drafts of client-facing work.

That does not make the draft ready for delivery.

Before substantive external work is delivered, it should be reviewed for strategic alignment, Brand Voice, AI patterns, evidence and claims, originality and source integrity, and human judgment.

The goal is not simply to remove obvious AI language.

The goal is to make sure the work reflects SimpliCreative's actual thinking.

## **15\. Using Examples**

The Second Brain may retrieve examples of strong previous work.

Examples are there to teach quality and reasoning.

They are not templates to copy.

A previous strategy, ad, website, presentation, proposal, or client deliverable may help us understand how a problem was approached.

It should not become the wording for the next client.

## **16\. Using External Frameworks**

The Second Brain may contain approved external methodologies and reference material.

These sources have boundaries.

For example, Ashley's Paid Ads Playbook may support paid-media execution and optimization.

It does not define SimpliCreative's positioning, ICP, offers, Brand Voice, commercial strategy, or overall acquisition strategy.

External expertise can strengthen our work.

It does not silently become SimpliCreative policy.

## **17\. Using the Second Brain for Meetings**

Before a meeting, use the Second Brain to retrieve relevant context such as:

Recent decisions.

Open questions.

Client priorities.

Project status.

Important evidence.

Previous commitments.

Potential risks.

Relevant scope.

After the meeting, Fathom and related workflows may help identify decisions, commitments, questions, observations, and follow-up.

Review consequential extracted information before relying on it.

## **18\. Using the Second Brain for Sales**

The Second Brain can help prepare for Executive Marketing Diagnostics and other sales conversations.

It may bring together Scorecard information, CRM history, approved public research, website evidence, previous interactions, and relevant SimpliCreative knowledge.

It can help us prepare.

It does not decide whether someone is a fit.

It does not turn a Scorecard result into a diagnosis.

It does not approve pricing.

It does not make commercial commitments.

Humans own those decisions.

## **19\. Using the Second Brain for Delivery**

During Blueprint, Foundation, Templated WordPress, and CARE engagements, the Second Brain can help retrieve the relevant SOP, assemble client context, organize evidence, prepare working materials, identify dependencies, preserve decisions, and support drafts.

Use the appropriate workflow rather than asking generic AI to invent a process.

The human SOP remains the process.

AI supports the process.

## **20\. Using the Second Brain for Acquisition**

The Second Brain may support:

Content research.

Thought leadership.

Campaign intelligence.

Paid-ad review.

Audience intelligence.

Search intelligence.

Partner intelligence.

Nurture.

Acquisition reporting.

Testing.

Learning.

It can recommend and draft.

Humans still approve external publication, material campaign decisions, and media spend.

## **21\. AI Drafts**

Treat AI-generated work as a draft unless the workflow explicitly tells you otherwise.

Review substantive work for:

Accuracy.

Evidence.

Strategic alignment.

Client context.

Brand Voice.

Claims.

Originality.

AI patterns.

Appropriate next action.

A polished draft can still contain bad reasoning.

## **22\. Human Approval**

Human approval is required for consequential decisions including:

Strategy.

Final client recommendations.

Fit and routing.

Pricing.

Scope.

Discounts.

Commercial exceptions.

Partner commitments.

Contracts.

External publication.

Paid creative.

Media spend.

Consequential production changes.

Client deliverables.

Legal or compliance-sensitive claims.

Changes to SimpliCreative knowledge.

The Second Brain should help humans make these decisions.

It should not remove humans from them.

## **23\. Correcting the Second Brain**

If the Second Brain gives you something that appears wrong:

Do not simply tell it the answer and assume the system is permanently fixed.

Determine whether the problem is:

Wrong source.

Old source.

Missing source.

Incorrect client context.

Bad data.

Incorrect interpretation.

Missing decision.

Unapproved knowledge.

Workflow error.

Retrieval failure.

The correction should happen at the right layer.

Otherwise the same problem may appear for someone else later.

## **24\. Teaching the Second Brain**

Do not treat every AI correction as something the entire agency should learn.

The system may identify a potential lesson.

A human determines whether it becomes durable SimpliCreative knowledge.

The learning sequence is:

`OBSERVATION`

↓

`INTERPRETATION`

↓

`HUMAN REFLECTION`

↓

`PATTERN CANDIDATE`

↓

`VALIDATION`

↓

`APPROVED LEARNING`

↓

`AGENCY KNOWLEDGE`

This prevents one client, one campaign, or one opinion from silently changing how the agency works.

## **25\. What Not to Do**

Do not ask AI to bypass a required human approval.

Do not use confidential information across clients.

Do not assume AI confidence equals evidence.

Do not turn one result into a universal rule.

Do not copy Gold-Standard or external reference language.

Do not use historical pricing as current pricing.

Do not treat external methodology as SimpliCreative strategy.

Do not let AI invent missing agency policy.

Do not publish substantive AI work without review.

Do not create separate unofficial versions of governing agency knowledge because you prefer different wording.

## **26\. When to Stop and Escalate**

Stop and involve the appropriate human when:

Client information appears in the wrong context.

Current sources conflict.

Required authority is missing.

Pricing is unclear.

Scope is unclear.

Evidence does not support the requested conclusion.

A legal or compliance-sensitive claim appears.

Source ownership or originality is uncertain.

AI recommends a consequential action outside its authority.

A workflow appears to be using an outdated process.

The system behaves differently from approved SimpliCreative policy.

## **27\. What Good Use Looks Like**

Good use of the Second Brain is not asking AI to think for us.

It is using AI so we can spend less time searching, reconstructing context, formatting, summarizing, and repeating work, and more time applying judgment.

The Second Brain should help the team move from:

`SEARCHING → KNOWING`

`REMEMBERING → RETRIEVING`

`RECONSTRUCTING → CONTINUING`

`ASSUMING → CHECKING`

`ACTIVITY → EVIDENCE`

`OBSERVATION → LEARNING`

## **28\. The Team Standard**

When you use the Second Brain, ask yourself:

Is it using the right client context?

Is it using current information?

Can I tell what is evidence versus interpretation?

Does the recommendation make sense?

Is this something AI is allowed to decide?

Does a human need to approve the next action?

If something feels wrong, investigate the source rather than simply polishing the output.

## **29\. Final Team Principle**

The Second Brain exists to make SimpliCreative's collective intelligence easier to access and use.

It should help us remember more without relying on individual memory.

It should help us move faster without sacrificing judgment.

It should help us become more consistent without becoming formulaic.

And it should help what we learn through client work compound over time without allowing every observation to become a rule.

The simplest way to remember the relationship is:

`AI RETRIEVES`

`AI CONNECTS`

`AI DRAFTS`

`AI FINDS PATTERNS`

`HUMANS INTERPRET`

`HUMANS DECIDE`

`HUMANS APPROVE`

`HUMANS TEACH THE SYSTEM WHAT BECOMES KNOWLEDGE`
