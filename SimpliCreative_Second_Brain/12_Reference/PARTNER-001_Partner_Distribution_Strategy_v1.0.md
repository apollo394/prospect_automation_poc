Status: Draft for Review

## **Purpose**

This strategy defines how SimpliCreative uses trusted partners as an inbound distribution channel for the Lean Marketing Assessment and related thought leadership.

The purpose is not to build a traditional referral program or outbound partner-sales motion.

The purpose is to extend SimpliCreative’s reach through organizations and individuals that already have the attention and trust of the marketing leaders we want to serve.

The governing principle is:

`PARTNERS EXTEND TRUST. SIMPLICREATIVE MUST STILL EARN THE CONVERSATION.`

The Lean Marketing Assessment remains the primary conversion point.

Partner distribution should help more qualified buyers encounter SimpliCreative’s point of view, recognize a connected marketing problem, and choose to assess where friction appears.

The core flow is:

`PARTNER AUDIENCE`

→ `PARTNER-DISTRIBUTED EDUCATION / CONTENT`

→ `LEAN MARKETING ASSESSMENT`

→ `HUBSPOT`

→ `PARTNER SOURCE ATTRIBUTION`

→ `EXECUTIVE MARKETING DIAGNOSTIC`

→ `QUALIFIED COMMERCIAL ROUTE`

This strategy adapts the useful partner-distribution principles from the attached Agency Lead Flow workbook, especially the ideas of reaching buyers through trusted intermediaries, prioritizing accessible/high-value partners, creating mutual-value offers, and making expectations explicit.

## **1\. Role in the Inbound Acquisition System**

Partner distribution is one of three primary inbound distribution engines:

| Distribution Engine | Primary Role |
| ----- | ----- |
| Owned | Build authority through SimpliCreative content, website, email, speaking, and searchable expertise |
| Paid | Amplify relevant messaging and capture existing intent |
| Partner | Reach qualified buyers through organizations and people they already trust |

All three should lead qualified buyers toward the same diagnostic journey.

`OWNED + PAID + PARTNER DISTRIBUTION`

↓

`LEAN MARKETING ASSESSMENT`

↓

`EXECUTIVE MARKETING DIAGNOSTIC`

↓

`APPROPRIATE COMMERCIAL ROUTE`

Partner distribution should strengthen the inbound system, not create a separate buyer journey.

## **2\. What Makes a Good Partner**

A strong partner has meaningful overlap with SimpliCreative’s ICP and a credible reason to share or collaborate around the Lean Marketing Assessment.

The best partners already serve, influence, advise, educate, or convene senior marketing and communications leaders in established service organizations.

A good partner should not be selected merely because they have a large audience.

Relevant factors include:

Audience fit.

Trust with the audience.

Complementary expertise.

Ease of access.

Mutual value.

Strategic fit.

Reputation.

Relationship quality.

Distribution capability.

Ability to collaborate without confusing ownership.

Potential for repeated activity.

## **3\. Priority Partner Categories**

| Partner Type | Why It Fits |
| ----- | ----- |
| PR Agencies | Their clients may need stronger website credibility, conversion, measurement, and downstream capture |
| Paid Media Agencies | They frequently encounter landing-page, messaging, conversion, or tracking problems outside their core scope |
| SEO / Search Specialists | They may identify website or messaging limitations beyond search execution |
| CRM / RevOps Consultants | They often see website-to-CRM, attribution, and measurement disconnects |
| Fractional CMOs | They diagnose broad marketing problems and may need trusted implementation partners |
| Brand Agencies | Their strategy and identity work often creates downstream website needs |
| Content Agencies | Their work may depend on stronger architecture, conversion, discoverability, or measurement |
| Associations | They aggregate marketing leaders within relevant industries |
| Professional Communities | They hold recurring attention and peer trust |
| Event Organizers | They can distribute useful diagnostic or educational experiences |
| Podcasts / Newsletters / Media | They already reach audiences interested in marketing leadership issues |
| Specialized Consultants | They may see connected problems outside their own delivery scope |

This list is a starting point, not a permanent ranking.

## **4\. Partner Fit Criteria**

Score potential partners against the criteria below.

| Criterion | Question |
| ----- | ----- |
| ICP Overlap | Do they regularly reach the kinds of organizations and buyers SimpliCreative serves? |
| Trust | Does their audience view them as a credible source or advisor? |
| Complementarity | Does their expertise naturally intersect with SimpliCreative without creating unnecessary competition? |
| Mutual Value | Can SimpliCreative provide something useful to them or their audience? |
| Ease of Access | Do we have a relationship, introduction path, or realistic way to start a conversation? |
| Distribution Ability | Can they realistically share content, host events, make introductions, or promote the Assessment? |
| Reputation | Would association strengthen rather than weaken SimpliCreative’s credibility? |
| Repeatability | Could the partnership produce ongoing value rather than a single isolated referral? |
| Operational Fit | Can both sides manage the collaboration without excessive friction? |

A partner does not need to score highly in every category.

The goal is to identify the strongest combination of audience relevance, trust, access, and mutual benefit.

## **5\. Partner Prioritization**

Start with the partners most likely to create useful momentum.

The attached workbook recommends prioritizing organizations that combine ease of access with mutual value rather than beginning with the most prestigious or difficult-to-reach relationships.

For SimpliCreative, I would use a simple internal score:

| Dimension | Score |
| ----- | ----- |
| Audience Fit | 1–5 |
| Trust / Credibility | 1–5 |
| Mutual Value | 1–5 |
| Ease of Access | 1–5 |
| Distribution Potential | 1–5 |
| Strategic Complementarity | 1–5 |

AI may calculate and rank the score.

Humans decide which partners to pursue.

Do not let a numerical score replace relationship judgment.

## **6\. Partner Segments**

Potential partners should be segmented according to how they can contribute to the inbound system.

| Segment | Primary Contribution |
| ----- | ----- |
| Referral Partner | Introduces qualified buyers |
| Content Partner | Co-creates or distributes thought leadership |
| Assessment Distribution Partner | Shares the Lean Marketing Assessment with its audience or clients |
| Event Partner | Hosts webinar, workshop, panel, or diagnostic event |
| Community Partner | Provides access to a member or peer network |
| Strategic Service Partner | Collaborates around complementary client needs |
| Media Partner | Provides editorial, podcast, newsletter, or audience exposure |

One partner may perform more than one role.

The role should be explicit.

## **7\. Partner Value Proposition**

Do not approach partners with:

“Can you send us leads?”

Lead with something useful to the partner and their audience.

The partnership should answer:

What are they trying to accomplish?

What does their audience need?

What can SimpliCreative contribute?

What does the partner gain?

What does SimpliCreative gain?

What is the smallest useful collaboration we can test?

The workbook’s emphasis on mutual benefit is especially relevant here.

## **8\. Partner Offer Models**

Potential collaboration models include:

| Model | Example |
| ----- | ----- |
| Partner-Distributed Assessment | Partner shares the Lean Marketing Assessment with clients or audience |
| Co-Branded Educational Session | Webinar on connected marketing problems |
| Partner Client Workshop | Private session for partner clients |
| Joint Thought Leadership | Article, video, guide, or panel |
| Diagnostic Event | Partner invites clients to complete Assessment and attend educational debrief |
| Newsletter Feature | Educational content plus Assessment CTA |
| Podcast / Interview | POV-driven conversation with contextual CTA |
| Referral Exchange | Appropriate introductions where there is genuine mutual fit |
| Client Resource | Partner includes Assessment in client resources or onboarding |
| Co-Marketing Campaign | Shared campaign around a common buyer problem |

Do not create a collaboration simply because it can be created.

The partner, audience, and problem should fit.

## **9\. Partner-Specific Assessment Positioning**

The Lean Marketing Assessment should remain structurally consistent.

The context in which a partner introduces it can change.

Examples:

| Partner | Possible Context |
| ----- | ----- |
| PR Agency | Is your digital foundation turning visibility into credibility, action, and measurable impact? |
| Paid Media Agency | Is the website supporting or suppressing the performance of paid traffic? |
| SEO Partner | Is stronger search visibility translating into meaningful buyer progress? |
| CRM Consultant | Is the website-to-CRM journey giving leadership a clear view of marketing contribution? |
| Fractional CMO | Where is the marketing system creating the most friction for a lean team? |
| Brand Agency | Is the new brand being translated into a digital experience buyers can understand, trust, and act on? |
| Association | How well is your marketing foundation supporting Discoverability, Credibility, Performance, and Agility? |

These are distribution angles.

They do not change the Assessment methodology.

## **10\. Partnership Outreach**

Partnership outreach should be human-led.

AI may support research and first-draft preparation.

A strong outreach message should establish:

Why this partner is relevant.

What audience or client overlap exists.

Why the collaboration could be useful.

What SimpliCreative can contribute.

What the proposed first step is.

The first ask should usually be small and specific.

Examples:

A short introductory conversation.

A co-hosted educational session.

A client-resource pilot.

A newsletter feature.

A limited Assessment distribution test.

Avoid over-designed proposals before there is evidence of mutual interest.

## **14\. Partner Distribution of the Assessment**

When a partner distributes the Lean Marketing Assessment, the buyer experience should remain inbound.

The prospect chooses to engage.

The partner should not imply that SimpliCreative has diagnosed the prospect in advance.

The distribution journey should be:

`PARTNER CONTENT / INTRODUCTION`

→ `LEAN MARKETING ASSESSMENT`

→ `SCOREAPP`

→ `HUBSPOT`

→ `PARTNER SOURCE ATTRIBUTION`

→ `PERSONALIZED RESULT`

→ `EXECUTIVE MARKETING DIAGNOSTIC WHEN APPROPRIATE`

The Assessment results remain governed by the same rules regardless of source.

`SCORECARD_RESULT != ROOT_CAUSE`

## **15\. HubSpot Attribution**

Partner distribution should be traceable.

Where technically practical, preserve:

Partner source.

Partner campaign.

Partner asset.

Assessment completion.

Assessment results.

Diagnostic booking.

Diagnostic outcome.

Opportunity.

Client conversion.

Relevant revenue or client value where appropriate.

Do not overwrite original first-touch attribution simply because a later partner interaction occurs.

The system should preserve both original acquisition source and meaningful partner influence where available.

## **17\. Lead Ownership and Client Protection**

Partner relationships should not create ambiguity about prospect or client ownership.

Before meaningful distribution or referral collaboration, clarify:

Who owns the existing client relationship?

Who communicates with the prospect?

What information can be shared?

What requires consent?

How introductions are handled.

Whether either party may market independently to introduced contacts.

How existing clients are protected.

How conflicts are handled.

How the partnership ends.

Do not assume alignment because both sides have good intent.

The workbook correctly emphasizes that unclear expectations create avoidable partnership friction.

## **18\. Commercial and Referral Arrangements**

Some partnerships may involve commercial referral arrangements.

Others may not.

Potential structures include:

No-fee reciprocal collaboration.

One-way referral fee.

Mutual referral fee.

Fixed co-marketing expense.

Revenue share.

Event sponsorship.

Other approved commercial arrangements.

Do not automatically introduce financial incentives.

The relationship should first make strategic sense.

Any commercial arrangement requires human approval and appropriate documentation.

AI may help draft internal options.

AI does not approve commercial terms.

## **19\. Partnership Agreements**

Material partnerships should have clear written expectations.

Depending on the relationship, address:

Commitments.

Deliverables.

Timing.

Frequency.

Audience use.

Lead handling.

Confidentiality.

Brand use.

Intellectual property.

Content ownership.

Data handling.

Commercial terms where applicable.

Liability boundaries.

Termination.

Exit expectations.

The attached workbook recommends keeping agreements simple while explicitly covering commitments, timing, IP, and exit terms.

Final agreement language should receive appropriate human and legal review where needed.

## **20\. Co-Branded Content**

Where a collaboration uses both brands, clarify:

Whose point of view is being expressed.

Who owns the content.

Who approves the content.

How each brand is represented.

Where the content can be reused.

What the CTA is.

What data each party receives.

How long the asset may remain live.

AI may draft co-marketing content from approved inputs.

Humans from both organizations approve final external materials.

## **21\. Partner Events**

For webinars, workshops, panels, or other events, define:

Audience.

Problem.

Learning objective.

Speakers.

Agenda.

Partner roles.

Promotion responsibilities.

Registration ownership.

Data-sharing rules.

Assessment CTA.

Follow-up.

Measurement.

A partner event should deliver useful insight even if no attendee becomes a SimpliCreative prospect.

The Assessment should be a logical next step, not a forced pitch.

## **22\. Partner Performance Measurement**

Do not evaluate partnerships only by number of introductions.

Measure progression.

| Layer | Question |
| ----- | ----- |
| Distribution | Did the partner actually reach the intended audience? |
| Engagement | Did the audience engage with the content or event? |
| Assessment | Did qualified people complete the Assessment? |
| Fit | Were those people aligned with the SimpliCreative ICP? |
| Diagnostic | Did qualified buyers progress to a Diagnostic? |
| Opportunity | Did appropriate commercial opportunities emerge? |
| Client | Did the partnership produce good-fit clients? |
| Value | Was the relationship worthwhile for both parties? |

A partner that creates fewer but stronger opportunities may be more valuable than one that generates high Assessment volume.

## **23\. Partner Review**

Active partnerships should be reviewed periodically.

Discuss:

What has happened?

What did each party commit to?

What worked?

What did not?

What did the audience respond to?

What did SimpliCreative learn?

What did the partner gain?

What should happen next?

Should the relationship expand, continue, pause, or end?

Do not maintain inactive partnerships indefinitely because they once seemed promising.

Preserve the relationship between partner activity and downstream client quality.

`PARTNER`

→ `COLLABORATION`

→ `AUDIENCE`

→ `CONTENT / EVENT / ASSESSMENT`

→ `ASSESSMENT COMPLETION`

→ `ICP FIT`

→ `DIAGNOSTIC`

→ `OPPORTUNITY`

→ `CLIENT`

→ `CLIENT QUALITY`

AI may identify patterns such as:

Which partner types generate the strongest fit?

Which partner audiences progress furthest?

Which collaboration types generate meaningful Assessment completions?

Which buyer problems resonate most through partner channels?

Which partners produce high engagement but low fit?

Which relationships create mutual value?

These observations inform decisions.

They do not automatically become agency strategy.

## **29\. Success Standard**

The partner distribution system is working when SimpliCreative is not simply accumulating referral relationships.

It is working when trusted partners have a clear reason to introduce SimpliCreative’s thinking to the right marketing leaders, their audiences receive genuine value, qualified buyers voluntarily enter the Lean Marketing Assessment, and the strongest relationships create repeatable value for both organizations.

The final test is not:

“Did this partner send us leads?”

It is:

“Did this partnership extend relevant trust to the right audience, create useful inbound demand, and produce qualified conversations without compromising the buyer experience, client relationships, or SimpliCreative’s strategic standards?”
