## **Purpose**

This SOP explains how SimpliCreative delivers a Templated WordPress engagement from qualification through launch and handoff.

Templated WordPress is a productized website implementation path for clients whose strategy is sufficiently established and whose website requirements can be responsibly implemented using an approved premium WordPress theme.

The engagement includes more than installing a theme.

SimpliCreative helps select an appropriate premium theme, develops the proposed sitemap, recommends the content structure for each page, maps those pages to suitable layouts within the selected theme, provides AI-assisted sample copy for client review, adapts the theme to the client’s brand, and implements the approved website.

It is not a smaller SimpliFoundation.

It is not the place to resolve substantial strategic uncertainty or accommodate requirements that fundamentally change the productized delivery model.

The governing principle is:

USE THE SIMPLEST IMPLEMENTATION PATH THAT RESPONSIBLY SOLVES THE PROBLEM.

The value of this offer depends partly on maintaining clear strategic, design, technical, and scope boundaries.

## **When Templated WordPress Is Appropriate**

A Templated WordPress engagement is appropriate when the client’s strategic foundation is sufficiently established and the website can be implemented responsibly within the productized model.

We should have enough clarity around the business objective, website objective, primary audience, positioning, core offers, general messaging direction, primary conversion actions, required functionality, relevant discoverability requirements, and measurement needs.

The client does not need to arrive with a finished sitemap or page-by-page content architecture.

SimpliCreative develops the proposed sitemap and content structure as part of the engagement.

The client is expected to provide the strategic foundation and brand assets required to make those recommendations responsibly.

## **When Templated WordPress Is Not Appropriate**

Do not force a project into the Templated WordPress model when it materially requires new positioning strategy, substantial ICP development, offer strategy, extensive messaging strategy, complex buyer-journey work, custom UX strategy, extensive custom information architecture, complex technical architecture, substantial custom functionality, major integrations, unresolved conversion strategy, major measurement strategy, extensive stakeholder alignment, substantial strategic research, or a highly customized visual system.

A client wanting the lower-cost offer does not make the engagement a template fit.

Likewise, a straightforward engagement should not be routed into SimpliFoundation simply because Foundation is the larger engagement.

Match the solution to the complexity.

## **Qualification Before Commercial Commitment**

Before confirming the Templated WordPress route, determine whether enough strategy exists to responsibly recommend and implement the website.

The team should be able to answer what the website needs to accomplish, who it serves, what the organization offers, how the company should generally be understood, what actions visitors should take, what important functionality is required, and whether the website can reasonably be created using a premium theme.

If unresolved strategic questions could materially change what should be built, stop and route the issue to the strategist.

Do not attempt to resolve substantial strategic uncertainty inside a productized implementation engagement.

## **Commercial Route**

Use the current approved Pricing & Profitability SOP to determine the commercial route, base investment, add-ons, and required approvals.

Do not create pricing from memory.

Do not compress Foundation-level complexity into the Templated WordPress scope simply to fit a target price.

## **Scope Before Delivery**

Before implementation begins, the project lead should confirm what the engagement includes and what has been added beyond the base productized scope.

Relevant scope areas may include pages, layouts, forms, integrations, blog functionality, resource or news content types, content migration, revision rounds, stakeholder meetings, rush requirements, and other approved additions.

The team should be able to distinguish base scope from approved additions before work begins.

## **Engagement Activation**

Before substantial work begins, confirm the executed agreement, approved scope, correct commercial route, approved add-ons, website objective, primary audience, established positioning and offers, available client content, required functionality, required integrations, measurement requirements, required access, client decision maker, and SimpliCreative project owner.

The client is expected to provide approved brand assets.

These may include logos, logo variations, brand colors, typography or font guidance, photography or imagery, existing brand guidelines, iconography or illustration guidance, and relevant marketing assets.

The client does not need to provide a completed sitemap or finished page architecture.

Developing the proposed sitemap and recommended page content structure is part of the Templated WordPress engagement.

## **Focused Kickoff**

The kickoff should confirm the implementation context without reopening the client’s entire marketing strategy.

Review what the website needs to accomplish, who it serves, the primary services or offers, existing website strengths and limitations, available content, required functionality, integrations, client-provided brand assets, known content or migration requirements, how premium theme selection will work, how the proposed sitemap and content outline will be developed, how feedback and approvals will work, client responsibilities, SimpliCreative responsibilities, scope boundaries, and timing dependencies.

The kickoff should provide enough context for SimpliCreative to research appropriate premium themes and recommend the site structure.

It should not become an unpaid Blueprint.

Simplified FAST Website Discovery Questionnaire

After kickoff, the client completes a simplified Website Discovery Questionnaire.

This questionnaire is intentionally lighter than the personalized Blueprint Questionnaire.

Its job is to collect and confirm enough information to support a productized website implementation.

It is not designed to perform deep strategic diagnosis.

Where reliable information already exists, the questionnaire should prefill the current understanding and ask the client to confirm, correct, or expand it.

Do not make the client repeat information SimpliCreative already has.

The questionnaire should generally collect information related to the FAST standard.

Flexible inputs may include who manages the website, how frequently content changes, landing-page needs, publishing requirements, current CMS frustrations, routine marketing tasks, and operational dependencies.

Accessible inputs may include known audience accessibility needs, client-supplied accessibility requirements, usability considerations, language requirements, and known organizational or legal requirements.

Strategic inputs may include website objective, primary audiences, positioning, services or offers, differentiation, buyer concerns, proof, credibility, competitor context, FAQs, desired visitor actions, content priorities, terminology preferences, existing messaging, and website expectations.

Trackable inputs may include primary conversions, secondary conversions, forms, lead qualification, CRM routing, analytics, campaign tracking, attribution requirements, reporting needs, and what leadership wants to understand after launch.

## **Questionnaire Review**

After questionnaire completion, review the responses before theme selection or sitemap generation.

Look for missing information, contradictions, new client corrections, implementation requirements, content gaps, and signs of strategic uncertainty.

If the questionnaire reveals substantial uncertainty around audience, positioning, offers, messaging direction, buyer journey, conversion strategy, or another issue that could materially change what should be built, stop and involve the strategist.

The Templated WordPress engagement should not quietly become a Blueprint.

## **Brand and Content Inputs**

Before theme research begins, confirm the client has provided the brand assets needed to evaluate and adapt potential themes.

Also collect relevant existing content and source materials, including service descriptions, website copy, brochures, case studies, testimonials, certifications, proof, downloadable resources, photography, videos, and other approved assets.

Some copy may still be incomplete at this stage.

SimpliCreative may develop a suggested content structure and AI-assisted sample copy from the validated client inputs.

If missing content reveals substantial positioning, audience, offer, or messaging uncertainty, stop and route the issue for strategic review.

## **Premium Theme Research**

SimpliCreative researches premium WordPress themes through the approved ThemeForest library.

The objective is not simply to find an attractive theme.

The selected theme should be appropriate for the client’s industry, brand, content needs, website objectives, functional requirements, expected page structure, and productized delivery model.

Consider visual fit, industry appropriateness, available page demos, available layouts and components, service and offer presentation, proof and credibility modules, CTA and conversion options, blog or resource functionality, responsiveness, CMS usability, technical quality, maintainability, compatibility requirements, and the amount of customization required.

Avoid themes that require substantial custom UX, custom development, or extensive design reconstruction to make them usable.

If suitable theme options cannot responsibly support the client’s needs, reassess whether the engagement still fits the Templated WordPress model.

## **Theme Shortlist**

SimpliCreative should identify a small number of viable premium theme options rather than asking the client to browse ThemeForest independently.

For each shortlisted theme, explain why it fits the organization, which parts of the theme are especially useful, how it could support the anticipated site structure, and any meaningful limitations.

Client visual preference matters, but SimpliCreative should make a clear recommendation based on strategic and implementation fit.

The final theme selection is human-approved.

The selected premium theme becomes the design and layout foundation for the engagement.

## **Proposed Sitemap**

Once sufficient questionnaire information exists and likely theme direction is understood, SimpliCreative develops the proposed sitemap.

The sitemap should consider the website objective, primary audiences, offers and services, existing strategy, buyer needs, conversion requirements, available content, discoverability requirements, proof requirements, existing website content worth preserving, and the practical layout capabilities of the selected theme.

The sitemap should not simply reproduce the current website.

It should represent SimpliCreative’s recommendation for how the established strategy should be organized into a practical website structure.

The sitemap should also stay within the productized nature of the engagement.

Do not introduce unnecessary strategic or architectural complexity.

## **Page-by-Page Content Outline**

After the sitemap is developed, SimpliCreative creates a suggested content outline for each material page.

The content outline should define what the page needs to communicate and the types of content that belong on the page.

Depending on the page, this may include page purpose, primary audience need, primary message, section kicker, recommended headline direction, supporting messages, service or offer information, buyer problems or concerns, differentiators, proof, credibility elements, process or approach, FAQs, primary CTA, secondary CTA, supporting resources, required imagery, SEO/AEO considerations, measurement considerations, and open client questions.

The content outline should give the client and delivery team enough direction to understand what belongs on each page without turning the engagement into a full custom messaging strategy.

## **Theme Layout Mapping**

The sitemap and content outline should map each major page and section to existing page demos, components, and layouts within the selected premium theme.

For each major page, identify the recommended theme demo or base layout, which existing sections should be used, which should be removed, which should be reordered, which client content belongs within each section, which components should be reused, and whether minor implementation adjustments are required.

The client’s strategy and content should guide the template selection.

The template should not dictate the strategy.

At the same time, the Templated WordPress model should work primarily within the selected theme’s existing layout and component system.

If a page requires substantial custom UX, an entirely new component system, or extensive custom design, pause and determine whether the requirement exceeds the productized scope.

## **AI-Assisted Sample Copy**

SimpliCreative provides initial sample copy as part of the page-by-page content outline.

AI may generate the first draft from validated questionnaire responses, approved client facts, existing content, approved proof, existing marketing materials, the page purpose, and applicable editorial guidance.

The sample copy should give the client something concrete to respond to rather than asking them to write every page from a blank document.

Sample copy may include section kickers, headlines, explanatory copy, service descriptions, benefit framing, proof framing, process language, CTA language, FAQs, and other appropriate page content.

AI-generated sample copy is not final website copy.

A human must review it for accuracy, strategic alignment, usefulness, voice, evidence, AI writing patterns, and originality before the client sees it.

Never invent claims, statistics, testimonials, credentials, service capabilities, qualifying criteria, legal statements, client outcomes, guarantees, or regulatory assertions.

## **Content Outline Deliverable**

The client-facing content-outline document should generally organize each section around three practical elements:

Layout Reference.

Sample Copy.

Client Revised Copy.

The Layout Reference shows the approved theme demo section or screenshot being used as the structural reference.

The Sample Copy contains SimpliCreative’s recommended first-draft language.

The Client Revised Copy gives the client a clear place to revise, clarify, or approve the content.

Theme demo screenshots are layout and design references only.

The theme’s sample text, headlines, images, colors, branding, claims, and messaging do not become client content.

## **Client Content and Structure Review**

Before substantial implementation begins, present the proposed sitemap, selected theme, page-by-page content structure, theme layout mapping, AI-assisted sample copy, and relevant assumptions to the client.

The purpose of this review is to align on the website structure and content direction before the team invests significant time in the build.

If client feedback introduces substantial new strategy, major custom UX, significant new functionality, or new complexity beyond the productized model, reassess the engagement before proceeding.

## **Approved Content Outline**

After client revisions are reconciled, the approved content outline becomes the implementation guide for the build.

Only approved content and layout decisions should feed production.

If copy remains pending, clearly identify what is approved, what is provisional, and what must still be resolved.

Build From the Approved Theme and Structure

After approval, configure the selected theme using the client’s brand assets, approved sitemap, approved content outline, approved copy, selected layouts, required functionality, measurement requirements, and relevant FAST requirements.

The team may adapt approved theme components to fit the client’s brand and content.

The engagement should primarily use existing theme layouts and components rather than designing a new custom visual system.

This is a key distinction from SimpliFoundation.

Templated WordPress selects, configures, and adapts an appropriate premium design system.

SimpliFoundation creates a custom implementation around the client’s approved strategy and requirements.

## **Protect the Productized Model**

The team should not casually introduce custom UX systems, one-off component libraries, complex animation, custom technical architecture, extensive custom layouts, unplanned integrations, custom design systems, or other work that materially changes the production assumptions behind the offer.

When a request exceeds the model, evaluate it before building it.

## **FAST Quality**

A Templated WordPress website should still meet SimpliCreative’s FAST standard.

Flexible means the client should have appropriate ability to manage routine content and use the system without unnecessary developer dependency.

Accessible means the implementation should support responsive, inclusive, and usable experiences. Automated accessibility tools may assist with preliminary checks but do not establish legal compliance.

Strategic means the site should support the established objectives, audience, messaging, credibility, discoverability, content needs, and conversion requirements.

Trackable means meaningful interactions included in scope should be measurable through the approved implementation.

Productized does not mean strategically careless.

## **Discoverability Implementation**

Implement the agreed SEO, AEO, GEO, metadata, schema, internal-linking, and related discoverability requirements included in scope.

Do not turn the engagement into an unscoped search strategy project.

If implementation exposes a material discoverability question that cannot be answered from existing strategy, route the issue for review.

## **Measurement Implementation**

Implement the tracking requirements included in scope.

Depending on the engagement, this may include analytics, tag management, form tracking, meaningful conversion events, UTM requirements, CRM handoffs, and other approved measurement requirements.

Do not promise attribution the client’s systems cannot support.

Installed analytics is not the same as meaningful measurement.

## **Client Review and Revisions**

Present the implemented experience within the boundaries of the approved engagement.

Distinguish between correction, in-scope revision, new preference, new content, new page, new layout, new functionality, new integration, and strategic change.

Use the revision rounds included in the agreement.

Consolidate client feedback where practical.

The designated client decision maker should resolve conflicting stakeholder feedback.

Additional revision rounds or additions should follow the approved commercial process.

## **Changing Complexity**

If the engagement begins to reveal Foundation-level complexity, stop and reassess.

Warning signs may include an unresolved audience, substantial positioning changes, offer restructuring, major sitemap strategy, custom UX requirements, content that cannot be finalized without deeper strategy, major stakeholder disagreement, significant new integrations, custom functionality, or materially more complex measurement requirements.

Do not hide complexity inside production.

## **Project Management**

Use monday.com for tasks, owners, deadlines, dependencies, execution status, and operational coordination.

Use the approved design, feedback, development, and content systems for their intended roles.

The Second Brain should preserve meaningful client and implementation intelligence.

It should not duplicate routine project activity.

## **Using AI**

AI may help retrieve approved client context, summarize questionnaire responses, identify missing inputs, support theme evaluation, generate proposed sitemap drafts, prepare page requirements, map content to approved layouts, generate sample copy, organize client feedback, compare implementation against scope, draft meeting summaries, support QA, and document meaningful decisions.

Humans remain responsible for final strategy, theme selection, sitemap approval, content approval, design judgment, technical architecture, scope, pricing, client commitments, QA, and launch.

Anything AI creates for client delivery receives human review first.

## **QA Preparation**

Before formal QA, confirm the approved pages and layouts are implemented, required content is present, approved forms and integrations are ready, responsive implementation is sufficiently complete, measurement is ready for validation, and known blockers are documented.

## **Strategic and Scope Fidelity**

Confirm that the finished site supports the stated website objective, speaks to the approved audience, preserves the approved positioning and messaging, represents the correct offers, includes the intended conversion actions, matches the approved page and layout scope, and has not accumulated unapproved custom requirements.

Resolve material discrepancies before launch.

## **Technical QA**

Depending on scope, validate responsive behavior, navigation, links, forms, integrations, content, imagery, analytics, events, metadata, schema, redirects, accessibility requirements, performance, browser/device behavior, error states, CMS functionality, and other approved technical requirements.

The appropriate human specialist owns final validation.

## **Launch** **Readiness**

Classify issues based on actual impact.

Blocking issues prevent required functionality or create unacceptable launch risk.

Material issues should normally be resolved before launch unless explicitly accepted.

Minor issues do not prevent launch and may be resolved according to project judgment.

Enhancements fall outside the required launch state or potentially outside scope.

The responsible human authorizes launch.

AI does not.

## **Post-Launch Validation**

After launch, validate the business-critical parts of the live website.

Depending on scope, this may include key pages, navigation, forms, CRM routing, analytics, conversion events, redirects, search accessibility, integrations, responsive behavior, and other critical functionality.

Do not assume successful deployment means everything is working.

## **Client Handoff**

At completion, make sure the client understands CMS access, routine editing, reusable components, technical maintenance, hosting, plugin or software responsibilities, analytics, content, measurement, ongoing optimization, known limitations, and support responsibilities.

Do not create unnecessary dependency on SimpliCreative for routine work the client should reasonably be able to manage.

## **Ongoing Support**

A Templated WordPress client may or may not be appropriate for SimpliCARE.

Do not automatically route every client into CARE.

Determine whether the current CARE offer genuinely fits the client’s ongoing needs.

## **Closeout**

Before closing the engagement, confirm the approved scope and add-ons are delivered, client approval is complete, included revisions are complete, strategic and scope fidelity has been checked, technical QA is complete, launch and critical post-launch validation are complete, known limitations are documented, client ownership is clear, durable client knowledge is updated, and any ongoing support route has been determined.

The engagement is not complete simply because WordPress is live.

It is complete when the agreed implementation has been delivered, validated, handed off, and closed cleanly.

## **What Is Worth Remembering**

Preserve durable client intelligence such as approved client facts, material implementation decisions, durable preferences, important corrections, scope changes, accepted limitations, important technical decisions, final launch state, ongoing ownership, and meaningful outcomes where later available.

Do not preserve every task, revision, content adjustment, Figma comment, MarkUp.io annotation, or routine implementation choice in the Second Brain.

Project activity belongs in the project system.

Durable context belongs in the intelligence system.

## **Success Standard**

The standard for Templated WordPress is not:

“How much customization can we squeeze into a productized website?”

It is:

“Can we give a strategically prepared client a high-quality FAST website by selecting and adapting the right premium design foundation, recommending the right site structure and content, and implementing it efficiently without allowing the engagement to quietly become a custom Foundation project?”
